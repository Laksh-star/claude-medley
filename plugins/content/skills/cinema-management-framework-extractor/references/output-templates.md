# Output Templates for Cinema Management Framework Extraction

Format specifications and structural templates for different output types.

## 1. Medium Article Format

McKee/Truby narrative structure, cultural bridges, technical credibility.

### Structure

**Opening (The Hook)**
- Start with a compelling scene from the film
- Pose a management question the scene raises
- Promise: What the reader will gain

**Part 1: The Setup (Cultural Bridge)**
- Introduce the film and its context
- Why this film matters for business lessons
- Cultural/historical significance if relevant (especially for Telugu films)
- Connect to readers' business challenges

**Part 2: The Framework (Technical Credibility)**
- Systematic analysis using specific lenses
- Multiple scene examples with evidence
- Management framework extraction
- Diagrams or visual models when helpful

**Part 3: The Application (Strategic Utility)**
- Real-world business applications
- Implementation guidelines
- Success metrics
- Cautionary notes

**Closing (The Transformation)**
- Return to opening scene/question
- Synthesize the lesson
- Call to action or reflection prompt
- Optional: Classical literature connection for depth

### Tone & Style

- **Voice**: Authoritative but accessible
- **Evidence**: Specific scenes with context
- **Balance**: 60% analysis, 40% application
- **Length**: 2000-3500 words
- **Sections**: Use clear headers
- **Quotes**: Use film dialogue sparingly but effectively

### Quality Checklist

- [ ] Hook grabs attention in first 100 words
- [ ] At least 3 specific scene citations
- [ ] Clear framework with 3-5 components
- [ ] Business application section with examples
- [ ] Cultural bridge appropriate to audience
- [ ] Technical credibility with frameworks
- [ ] No generic "movies teach us" platitudes

## 2. Workshop Case Study Format

Interactive format designed for facilitation with discussion and participant engagement.

### Structure

**Cover Sheet**
- Film title, year, director
- Industry/context relevance
- Key themes to be explored
- Suggested audience level
- Time required (typically 45-90 minutes)

**Pre-Work (Optional)**
- Film viewing instructions
- Specific scenes to watch
- Initial reflection questions

**Case Study Narrative**
- Film synopsis (200-300 words)
- Focus on plot elements relevant to management lesson
- Character roster with roles
- Key decision points highlighted

**Scene Analysis Sections**

For each key scene:

**Scene Description**
- Timestamp or sequence location
- Context within film
- Characters involved
- What happens (objective description)

**Discussion Questions**
- 3-5 questions per scene
- Mix of analytical and application questions
- Gradual progression from observation to judgment
- Example format:
  - What did [character] do? (Observation)
  - Why did they make that choice? (Analysis)
  - What were the consequences? (Evaluation)
  - What would you do differently? (Application)
  - How does this apply to your organization? (Transfer)

**Management Framework Section**
- Clear articulation of principle
- Supporting evidence from multiple scenes
- Business parallels
- Implementation guidelines

**Activity Suggestions**
- Role-playing scenarios
- Decision-making simulations
- Framework application exercises
- Group debate topics

**Facilitator Notes**
- Time allocation guidance
- Expected insights/responses
- Common misconceptions to address
- Transition prompts between sections

**Takeaway Template**
- Structured reflection worksheet
- Action planning format
- Commitment mechanism
- Follow-up activities

### Tone & Style

- **Voice**: Facilitator-friendly, neutral prompts
- **Questions**: Open-ended, thought-provoking
- **Balance**: 40% content, 60% activities/questions
- **Length**: 8-15 pages
- **Formatting**: Clear sections, easy navigation

### Quality Checklist

- [ ] Clear learning objectives stated upfront
- [ ] Mix of individual and group activities
- [ ] Questions progress from simple to complex
- [ ] Timing guidelines for each section
- [ ] Facilitator notes provide guidance
- [ ] Action planning component included
- [ ] Materials list if props/handouts needed

## 3. Movisvami Short-Form Format

Concise insight extraction focused on life lessons over pure business, accessible and emotionally resonant.

### Structure

**The Scene** (100-150 words)
- Vivid description of a key moment
- Sensory details
- Emotional tone
- Character's dilemma or choice

**The Insight** (150-250 words)
- What this moment reveals
- The life/business principle
- Why it matters
- Universal human truth behind it

**The Application** (200-300 words)
- How to apply in life/work
- Specific, actionable steps
- Personal reflection prompt
- Avoid jargon, use accessible language

**The Resonance** (50-100 words)
- Closing reflection
- Emotional or philosophical note
- Optional: Classical literature parallel
- Leave reader with feeling, not just thinking

### Tone & Style

- **Voice**: Personal, reflective, warm
- **Language**: Simple, evocative, minimal jargon
- **Balance**: 30% scene, 30% insight, 30% application, 10% resonance
- **Length**: 500-800 words total
- **Paragraphs**: Short, readable
- **Accessibility**: For general audience, not just business professionals

### Quality Checklist

- [ ] Opens with vivid, specific scene
- [ ] Insight feels earned, not imposed
- [ ] Application is concrete and actionable
- [ ] Emotional resonance throughout
- [ ] Accessible to non-business readers
- [ ] Can be read in 3-4 minutes
- [ ] Social media shareable (key quote extractable)

## 4. Framework Document Format

Structured management framework with diagram-ready format and implementation focus.

### Structure

**Framework Overview**
- Name of framework
- One-sentence essence
- Origin (film and scenes)
- Applicable contexts

**Core Components**

For each component (typically 3-5):

**Component Name**: Clear, memorable label

**Definition**: What it means (2-3 sentences)

**Film Evidence**: Specific scene demonstrating this component
- Scene description
- Character actions
- Outcome

**Business Parallel**: Real-world analog
- Industry examples
- Common manifestations
- Why it matters

**Indicators**: Observable signs of this component
- Behavioral markers
- Outcome measures
- Warning signs when absent

**Implementation Guidance**

**Prerequisites**
- Organizational readiness factors
- Required resources
- Stakeholder alignment needed

**Step-by-Step Process**
1. Detailed action steps
2. Numbered and sequential
3. With expected outcomes at each stage

**Integration with Existing Systems**
- Where this fits in current processes
- Complementary frameworks
- Potential conflicts to manage

**Measurement & Evaluation**

**Success Metrics**
- Quantitative indicators
- Qualitative indicators
- Timeline for assessment
- Benchmarks

**Quality Standards**
- What "good" looks like
- Common failure modes
- Adjustment triggers

**Stakeholder Analysis**

**Beneficiaries**
- Who gains from this framework
- Nature of benefits
- Incentive alignment

**Potential Resisters**
- Who might resist
- Sources of resistance
- Mitigation strategies

**Cautionary Notes**

**When NOT to Use This Framework**
- Contexts where it doesn't apply
- Risk factors
- Prerequisite conditions

**Limitations**
- What this framework doesn't address
- Complementary frameworks needed
- Scope boundaries

**Common Pitfalls**
- Typical implementation errors
- Misinterpretation patterns
- How to avoid them

**Visual Representation**
- Diagram-ready description
- Suggested visual format (flowchart, matrix, cycle, etc.)
- Key relationships to illustrate
- Optional: ASCII diagram for structure

### Tone & Style

- **Voice**: Professional, systematic, authoritative
- **Language**: Precise terminology, defined concepts
- **Balance**: 20% film evidence, 80% business framework
- **Length**: 5-10 pages
- **Formatting**: Structured, scannable, numbered/bulleted

### Quality Checklist

- [ ] Framework has 3-5 clear components
- [ ] Each component linked to film evidence
- [ ] Implementation steps are actionable
- [ ] Success metrics are measurable
- [ ] Stakeholder analysis included
- [ ] Cautionary notes prevent misuse
- [ ] Visual representation possible
- [ ] Can stand alone as reference document

## General Output Guidelines

### Across All Formats

**Evidence Standards**
- Specific scene references (not vague "in the film")
- Character names and actions
- Dialogue when relevant (sparingly)
- Contextual setup

**Voice Consistency**
- Authoritative but not academic
- Analytical but not cold
- Accessible but not simplistic
- Confident but acknowledging limitations

**Cultural Sensitivity**
- When using Telugu films: provide context for non-Telugu readers
- When using Western films for Indian audience: cultural translation
- Avoid assumptions about universal interpretation
- Acknowledge cultural boundedness when relevant

**Quality Standards**
- No plot summaries masquerading as analysis
- No generic "movies are inspiring" content
- No business jargon without explanation
- No frameworks without film evidence

### Editing Checklist

Before finalizing any output:
1. **Specificity check**: Every claim backed by scene reference?
2. **Actionability check**: Can reader actually apply this?
3. **Evidence check**: Multiple examples supporting each point?
4. **Voice check**: Consistent with "Directing Business" style?
5. **Cultural check**: Context provided when needed?
6. **Quality check**: Meets all format-specific checklist items?
